Mathematical Foundations & Architectural Whitepaper

Exhaustive mathematical formulation and algorithmic derivation of the OmniPulse CLV & Churn platform.

1. Probabilistic Transaction Modeling: BG/NBD (Fader, Hardie & Lee)

In non-contractual and hybrid SaaS environments, customers do not explicitly announce their cancellation; they simply become inactive. The Beta-Geometric / Negative Binomial Distribution (BG/NBD) model solves this through a compound latent process:

  • Transaction Process: While active, each customer makes purchases according to a Poisson process with transaction rate \(\lambda\). Across customers, \(\lambda\) is heterogeneous and follows a Gamma distribution with shape \(r\) and scale \(\alpha\):
    f(\lambda | r, \alpha) = \frac{\alpha^r \lambda^{r-1} e^{-\alpha \lambda}}{\Gamma(r)}
  • Dropout Process: After each transaction, a customer may become inactive with unobserved probability \(p\). Across the population, \(p\) follows a Beta distribution with parameters \(a\) and \(b\):
    f(p | a, b) = \frac{p^{a-1} (1-p)^{b-1}}{B(a, b)}
  • Conditional Expectation \(E[Y(t)|x, t_x, T]\): The expected number of future transactions in interval \((T, T+t]\) given observed history (frequency \(x\), recency \(t_x\), customer tenure \(T\)) is:
    E[Y(t) | r, \alpha, a, b, x, t_x, T] = \frac{a + b + x - 1}{a - 1} \cdot \left[ 1 - \left(\frac{\alpha + T}{\alpha + T + t}\right)^{r+x} {}_2F_1\left(r+x, b; a+b+x-1; \frac{t}{\alpha + T + t}\right) \right] \cdot P(\text{Alive})

2. Monetary Value Submodel: Gamma-Gamma with Bayesian Shrinkage

To decouple purchase frequency from order value, the Gamma-Gamma model assumes customer monetary value per transaction \(m_i\) follows a Gamma distribution with shape \(p\) and scale \(\nu_i\). The individual parameter \(\nu_i\) itself varies across the population according to a Gamma prior \(\text{Gamma}(q, \gamma)\).

E[M | p, q, \gamma, x, \bar{m}_x] = \left(\frac{q - 1}{p x + q - 1}\right) \frac{\gamma p}{q - 1} + \left(\frac{p x}{p x + q - 1}\right) \bar{m}_x

This creates an optimal Bayesian shrinkage estimator: for customers with few transactions (\(x \to 0\)), their expected spend shrinks toward the population mean prior. For high-frequency customers (\(x \gg 1\)), their individual observed average \(\bar{m}_x\) dominates.

3. Continuous Discounted Customer Equity

Combining the BG/NBD transaction expectation and Gamma-Gamma expected spend over planning horizon \(H\) (typically 12 or 24 months) discounted at rate \(d\) with gross margin \(m\):

\text{CLV}_H = \sum_{t=1}^{H} \frac{E[Y(t) - Y(t-1) | x, t_x, T] \cdot E[M] \cdot \text{Gross Margin}}{(1 + d)^t}

4. Causal ML & Uplift Modeling vs Standard Churn Scoring

Standard churn models predict \(P(\text{Churn} = 1 | X)\). However, optimizing retention spend requires answering a counterfactual causal question: What is the incremental uplift of an intervention \(T \in \{0, 1\}\)?

\tau(X) = E[Y | X, T=1] - E[Y | X, T=0]
1. Persuadables
Churn if untreated (\(Y_0=1\)), Retain if treated (\(Y_1=0\)). All retention budget must target this segment!
2. Sure Things
Retain regardless (\(Y_0=0, Y_1=0\)). Budget spent here is 100% wasted margin dilution.
3. Lost Causes
Churn regardless (\(Y_0=1, Y_1=1\)). Expensive white-glove efforts will not change outcome.
4. Sleeping Dogs
Retain if left alone (\(Y_0=0\)), but contact prompts cancellation (\(Y_1=1\))! Never disturb.

5. Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) Attribution

To ensure zero black-box opacity for executive customer success teams, every individual churn probability is decomposed using Shapley values rooted in cooperative game theory:

\phi_i(v) = \sum_{S \subseteq N \setminus \{i\}} \frac{|S|!(|N| - |S| - 1)!}{|N|!} \left[ v(S \cup \{i\}) - v(S) \right]

This proves mathematically that the sum of local feature attributions exactly equals the difference between the customer's predicted risk and the baseline expected population risk: \(f(x) = \phi_0 + \sum_{i=1}^M \phi_i\).
